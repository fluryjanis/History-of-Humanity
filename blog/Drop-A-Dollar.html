How the Historical Value of Money is Calculated

Money does not possess a fixed intrinsic scale. Its value is determined by what a given quantity of currency can buy in goods and services at a specific moment in human history. Over decades, general price levels change due to economic output, industrial technology, raw material supply, and changes in the volume of currency in circulation.

The Consumer Price Index Formula

To calculate the modern equivalent of historical currency, economists use the official Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U):

Standard CPI Conversion Formula Modern Equivalent = Historical Amount × (Current CPI / Historical CPI)

For example, when looking at December 1973, the official CPI-U was 46.20. By August 2026, the index reached 334.980. The purchasing power ratio is calculated as:

$1.00 × (334.980 / 46.20) = $7.2506... ≈ $7.25

Why Monthly Data Instead of Daily?

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics surveys prices across thousands of retail establishments, rental units, and service providers each month. It aggregates these observations into a single certified monthly number. Because daily CPI measurements do not exist in official government datasets, picking any date within a month applies that month's certified CPI value.

The 2026 Anchor

Because the year 2026 is incomplete, calculations do not guess an arbitrary full-year average. Instead, all modern comparisons use the latest published official release: August 2026 (CPI-U: 334.980), published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics on September 11, 2026.
