How the Historical Value of Money is Calculated

Money is an invention whose value shifts with the size of an economy, the volume of currency in circulation, and the productivity of human industry. Over decades, currency values dilute through consumer price inflation.

The Consumer Price Index Formula

This calculator uses the CPI-U U.S. city average, all items, not seasonally adjusted:

Purchasing Power Formula Modern Equivalent = Historical Amount × (Current CPI / Historical CPI)

For example, in December 1973, the official CPI-U stood at 46.20. By August 2026, the index reached 334.980. The purchasing power ratio is calculated as:

$1.00 × (334.980 / 46.20) = $7.2506... ≈ $7.25

Handling 2026 Data

Because 2026 is currently in progress, calculations do not guess an arbitrary full-year average. Instead, all conversions use the latest certified monthly figure: August 2026 (CPI-U: 334.980), released September 11, 2026 by the Bureau of Labor Statistics.

Authoritative Data Reference U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Consumer Price Index (CPI-U), Series CUUR0000SA0 (1913–August 2026)

A dollar is not a permanent unit of human labor or material wealth. It is a measurement of value that is continuously rewritten by time.
