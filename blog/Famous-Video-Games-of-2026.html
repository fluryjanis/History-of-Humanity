Famous Video Games of 2026

A record of the individual video games that people played, discussed, streamed, bought, and remembered during 2026.

Published September 3, 2026 · Looking at video games in 2026

Video games were not one thing in 2026.

People played enormous multiplayer games that had existed for years, waited for major releases, discovered tiny independent games through social media, returned to old favorites, watched other people play, modified games with community-created content, and sometimes spent more time talking about a game than actually playing it.

This article records the individual games that helped make up that landscape.

It is not a ranking of the "best" games of 2026. That would represent only one interpretation of the year. Instead, this is an attempt to preserve the games that were visible in the culture of 2026 — whether because they were enormous commercial releases, unexpected indie hits, viral games, long-running communities, major franchises, or games that people simply could not stop talking about.

Because this article was written during 2026, the record is necessarily incomplete. Some games released earlier in the year have already had months to build an audience, while others are only beginning to appear. The article can therefore be updated as the year becomes history.

How Player Numbers Are Recorded

One of the easiest numbers to find for a modern video game is its concurrent player count: the number of people playing at the same time.

For PC games released on Steam, historical Steam concurrent-player data provides one useful record of how large a game's PC audience became. SteamDB preserves historical peaks, while Steam itself publishes platform-wide player charts.

Important: A Steam concurrent-player peak is not the total number of people who played a game. It does not include players on PlayStation, Xbox, Nintendo platforms, mobile devices, other PC storefronts, or people who played at different times. It is simply a measurable record of simultaneous Steam activity.

When a Steam peak is included below, it should therefore be read as a historical measurement of the game's Steam audience rather than a claim about its total worldwide player base.

20 Games That Defined 2026

These twenty games form the featured part of the archive. They represent some of the major releases and gaming stories associated with 2026. Inclusion does not mean that one game was "better" than another.

1. Slay the Spire 2

Mega Crit's sequel to the influential deck-building roguelike became one of the largest PC launches of the year.

Steam all-time peak: 574,638 concurrent players.

2. Resident Evil Requiem

Capcom's next major Resident Evil release continued the long-running survival-horror series with another large-scale entry.

Steam all-time peak: 344,214 concurrent players.

3. Subnautica 2

The sequel to the underwater survival game became one of the year's largest Steam launches.

Steam all-time peak: 467,582 concurrent players.

4. Forza Horizon 6

Microsoft's open-world racing series returned with another major installment, attracting a large PC audience.

Steam all-time peak: 302,645 concurrent players.

5. Crimson Desert

The large-scale action RPG became one of the year's most visible Korean-developed releases.

Steam all-time peak: 276,261 concurrent players.

6. Marathon

Bungie's return to the Marathon universe arrived as a multiplayer extraction shooter and became one of the year's major releases.

Steam all-time peak: 88,337 concurrent players.

7. Monster Hunter Stories 3: Twisted Reflection

Capcom expanded its Monster Hunter Stories series with another role-playing adventure built around monster collection.

Steam all-time peak: 54,778 concurrent players.

8. Nioh 3

Team Ninja returned to its demanding action-RPG series with a third mainline Nioh game.

Steam all-time peak: 88,045 concurrent players.

9. The Blood of Dawnwalker

A dark fantasy action RPG from Rebel Wolves, released in September, became one of the final major releases to enter the gaming landscape covered by this early record.

Steam peak recorded by September 3: 64,438 concurrent players.

10. DEATH STRANDING 2: ON THE BEACH

Hideo Kojima's unusual post-apocalyptic series continued with another journey built around exploration, delivery, connection, and isolation.

Steam all-time peak: 57,682 concurrent players.

11. Paralives

The life simulation game became notable for its attempt to provide an alternative to the established life-simulation market.

Steam all-time peak: 78,603 concurrent players.

12. MOUSE: P.I. For Hire

A first-person shooter inspired by early cartoon animation and noir detective fiction.

Steam all-time peak: 13,755 concurrent players.

13. Pokémon Pokopia

A Pokémon spin-off built around exploration, construction, and a different interpretation of the Pokémon world.

No comparable Steam player figure is recorded because the game is not represented by a Steam release.

14. 007 First Light

A new James Bond adventure brought the long-running spy franchise into another generation of video games.

15. Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced

The return of one of the most recognizable Assassin's Creed settings connected a major modern franchise with one of its best-known earlier adventures.

16. Mewgenics

A strategy and creature-management game from the creators of The Binding of Isaac and other distinctive indie titles.

17. Deep Rock Galactic: Rogue Core

The Deep Rock Galactic universe expanded into a new cooperative roguelike experience.

18. LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight

Batman returned to the LEGO game format in another family-oriented interpretation of the character.

19. Mina the Hollower

Yacht Club Games' retro-inspired action adventure drew on the visual language of classic handheld and 8-bit-era games.

Steam all-time peak: 12,916 concurrent players.

20. John Carpenter's Toxic Commando

A cooperative first-person shooter combining action, zombies, and the distinctive horror identity associated with John Carpenter.

Games That Were More Than Games

Some games in 2026 were difficult to describe simply as games.

Minecraft, Roblox, and Fortnite had developed into enormous platforms for creation, communication, entertainment, and social interaction. People did not only play them according to rules created by a developer. They created their own experiences inside them.

Minecraft remained a sandbox where people built, explored, survived, created servers, made videos, and modified the game.

Roblox functioned as a huge collection of user-created experiences, with games inside a platform that was itself becoming a social and creative environment.

Fortnite continued to exist somewhere between game, entertainment platform, social space, and digital event venue.

How People Played Games in 2026

The games themselves tell only part of the story.

In 2026, people played games on desktop computers, laptops, consoles, handheld gaming PCs, phones, tablets, and other connected devices.

Steam's continued growth also demonstrated the importance of PC gaming platforms. By September, Steam's ecosystem contained an enormous number of games, while its live charts regularly showed tens of millions of simultaneous users across the platform.

Handheld PC gaming had also become increasingly normal. A game no longer necessarily had to be designed around sitting at a desk. The same library could move between a traditional computer and a handheld device.

Meanwhile, watching games remained an important part of gaming culture. YouTube videos, livestreams, short-form clips, speedruns, challenge videos, reviews, reactions, guides, and game-development videos all influenced which games people discovered.

Gaming Was Also a Spectator Culture

In previous generations, playing a game generally meant sitting down and playing it yourself.

By 2026, that definition was incomplete.

Millions of people encountered games through people who were playing them. A person could become familiar with a game without ever purchasing it, simply by watching someone else explore it.

This changed what made a game successful. Games increasingly had to work not only as experiences for players, but also as things that could be watched, discussed, clipped, shared, and turned into stories.

This is one reason relatively small games could suddenly become visible to enormous audiences.

The Importance of Old Games

One of the clearest differences between modern games and many older forms of entertainment is persistence.

A movie from 1980 can still be watched. A book from 1880 can still be read. But video games depend on hardware, operating systems, storefronts, servers, controllers, patches, licenses, and companies continuing to support them.

Yet many games from previous generations remained active in 2026.

Terraria, Stardew Valley, Minecraft, Team Fortress 2, The Sims 4, Counter-Strike, Dota 2, and many others continued to attract players long after their original releases.

This means that the history of gaming cannot be written only as a list of yearly releases. The games of 2026 included games created in 2026, games created years earlier, and games that had become permanent parts of people's digital lives.

A Catalog, Not a Ranking

There is no single game that represents 2026.

One person might remember Slay the Spire 2. Another might remember Minecraft. Someone else might remember spending hundreds of hours in Counter-Strike 2, playing Roblox with friends, discovering a strange indie game through a video, or waiting for Grand Theft Auto VI.

All of those experiences belong to the same year.

That is why this archive does not attempt to decide which games were objectively the most important. Instead, it preserves a collection of them.

Future readers may find some of these names immediately familiar. Others may be completely forgotten. That is part of the purpose.

A historical archive should preserve things before we know which things will become famous.

What 2026's Games Tell Us

The video games of 2026 reveal a medium that had become simultaneously enormous and fragmented.

There were giant franchises with hundreds of thousands of simultaneous players. There were tiny independent games discovered almost entirely through online communities. There were games designed to be played for ten minutes and games designed to occupy someone's life for thousands of hours.

There were games people bought, games people streamed, games people watched, games people modified, games people created inside other games, and games people remembered primarily because of the communities that formed around them.

That diversity is important.

When people in the future look back at 2026, they should not see only the biggest releases. They should be able to see the strange games, forgotten games, viral games, unfinished games, multiplayer games, independent games, childhood games, and games that people simply kept playing because they liked them.

A Record Still Being Written

This page was created on September 3, 2026.

The year is not over.

More games will be released. Some games listed here will become much bigger. Others will disappear from public attention. A game that seems minor today may become one of the defining games of the year by December.

That uncertainty is not a flaw in the archive.

It is part of what makes this a historical record.

One day, these games will be history.

The games people played in 2026 will eventually become evidence of how people spent their free time, what kinds of stories interested them, what technologies they used, what communities they joined, and what they considered fun.

Some will be remembered.

Some will be forgotten.

This archive exists to preserve both.
