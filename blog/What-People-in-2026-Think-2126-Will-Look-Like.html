What People in 2026 Think 2126 Will Look Like

A snapshot of the future imagined from the beginning of the second century of the modern digital age.

Published September 1, 2026 · Looking ahead 100 years

In 2026, the year 2126 is almost impossible to imagine. A hundred years separates us from a world that would be nearly unrecognizable to someone living in 1926.

Yet people in 2026 are constantly imagining what the next century might bring.

Some visions are optimistic. Some are frightening. Some are highly technical. Others are closer to science fiction.

This article is not an attempt to decide which vision is correct. It is a record of the ideas people are discussing and developing in 2026.

Archive note The purpose of this article is to preserve the way people in 2026 imagined the future.

The sources below are included so that future readers can see the ideas, research, projects, and predictions that existed at the time.

1. Artificial Intelligence Becomes Part of Everything

Few technologies are shaping people's ideas about the future more strongly than artificial intelligence.

In 2026, AI is already being used for writing, programming, research, image generation, education, customer service, scientific work, and countless other tasks.

The major question is what happens if these systems continue becoming more capable.

One common vision is not simply "better chatbots." It is AI becoming a general layer of infrastructure used throughout society.

People imagine AI systems that can conduct scientific research, operate businesses, teach students individually, design products, manage complicated organizations, and perform many forms of intellectual work with little human supervision.

Predictions about the timing vary dramatically. Some researchers and futurists expect extremely capable systems within decades, while others are much more cautious.

A 2017 survey of machine-learning researchers, for example, found substantial disagreement about when AI might outperform humans across different tasks, while respondents assigned a median 50% probability to AI outperforming humans at every task within 45 years.

Example source from the era When Will AI Exceed Human Performance? — Survey of AI researchers

By 2126, today's AI may look primitive. Or the development of AI may have taken a direction nobody in 2026 expected.

What matters for this archive is that people living in 2026 were already imagining a world where artificial intelligence could become one of the central technologies of civilization.

2. Humans Live Beyond Earth

Another major vision of 2126 is that Earth will no longer be the only place where humans permanently live and work.

This idea is unusual because parts of it are no longer purely speculative.

NASA's current Moon-to-Mars architecture is explicitly designed around developing the capabilities needed for long-term human exploration of the Moon, Mars, and beyond.

What exists in 2026 NASA — Moon to Mars Architecture

The envisioned lunar infrastructure is surprisingly concrete. NASA describes the need for reliable power, water, landing pads, roads, construction systems, and technologies capable of extracting useful materials from the lunar surface.

Example: lunar infrastructure NASA — Lunar Surface Technology

That includes extracting lunar resources to produce water and oxygen, autonomous construction equipment, and infrastructure built from lunar material.

By 2126, the more ambitious versions of this idea imagine permanent lunar settlements, industrial activity in space, and human settlements on Mars.

NASA's current architecture even includes specific planning for landing humans and equipment on Mars and eventually establishing a human presence there.

Example: Mars planning NASA — Moon to Mars Architecture Components

Space could become an industrial environment

The vision goes beyond simply putting people on another planet.

Some people imagine that resources from the Moon and asteroids will eventually be used to build things in space.

NASA is already researching technologies for extracting resources from lunar soil, including water and other materials that could support propulsion, energy production, and life support.

Example: lunar resources NASA — Lunar Resource-Seeking Technologies

Asteroid mining is another long-running idea. Proposed systems involve using robots to locate and extract water, metals, and other resources from asteroids.

Example: asteroid resources NASA — Robotic Asteroid Prospector

3. Cities Adapt to a Changing Planet

Another major vision of the future is less about leaving Earth and more about changing the places where humans already live.

Climate change is expected to affect cities through increasing heat, flooding, sea-level rise, water stress, and changes in extreme weather.

That creates a very concrete question: What does a city built for a warmer world actually look like?

Ideas being discussed today include redesigned urban infrastructure, cooler walking routes, expanded urban agriculture, more resilient electricity networks, and buildings and neighborhoods designed around extreme heat.

Example from 2026 The Wall Street Journal — How Cities Can Adapt to Warmer Climates

Other visions are much more dramatic.

Coastal cities could require enormous flood defenses. Some areas could be elevated or redesigned around water. Other locations could eventually become too expensive or difficult to protect.

Long-term sea-level projections demonstrate why this possibility appears in future thinking. The IPCC has assessed sea-level rise continuing for centuries and millennia, with substantially larger rises possible under higher-emissions scenarios.

Scientific background IPCC — Special Report on the Ocean and Cryosphere

The city of 2126 therefore might not simply be a more technologically advanced version of today's city. It could be physically redesigned around the environmental conditions of the century.

4. Humans Live Longer and Stay Healthier

A fourth major idea is that medicine could fundamentally change aging and the length of healthy human life.

This prediction exists in several different forms.

One version is relatively conservative: better prevention, personalized medicine, improved cancer treatments, replacement organs, and therapies that allow people to remain healthy for more of their existing lifespan.

A more ambitious version imagines directly targeting the biological mechanisms of aging.

Researchers are currently studying areas including cellular reprogramming, senolytics, biological age measurements, and other approaches intended to understand or alter the aging process.

Example from 2026 Nature Medicine — Biological aging clocks in health and disease

But there is significant disagreement about how far this can go. Current scientific literature does not establish that humans will routinely live for centuries.

That uncertainty is itself part of the 2026 record.

Another 2026 perspective Nature — How long can humans live? We simply don't know

The most extreme visions of 2126 therefore imagine humans living dramatically longer lives, while more cautious visions simply expect people to remain healthy much later into old age.

5. Humans and Machines Become More Closely Connected

Another major vision concerns the boundary between people and technology.

Today, most interaction with computers still happens through screens, keyboards, mice, microphones, cameras, and touchscreens.

Future visions imagine that boundary becoming much less noticeable.

Brain-computer interfaces could allow people to control computers using neural signals. Advanced prosthetics could restore or enhance physical abilities. Augmented reality could place digital information directly into a person's view of the physical world.

In some visions, computers would respond to human intentions without requiring traditional interfaces at all.

Example of a 2026 prediction The Wall Street Journal — Five Amazing Tech Innovations We Should Expect

That same 2026 discussion included predictions that brain-computer interfaces could eventually become as common as smartphones.

Other predictions are less extreme: interfaces may simply become easier, more natural, and more integrated into everyday devices.

6. Robots Become Ordinary

The future of robotics is another major part of the 2126 imagination.

Instead of robots being unusual machines found mostly in factories, people imagine them becoming ordinary parts of homes, hospitals, farms, warehouses, construction sites, and public infrastructure.

The important shift is already beginning in 2026.

Autonomous robots are operating in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and other real-world environments, while researchers and companies are working toward machines capable of operating in less controlled environments.

Example from 2026 World Economic Forum — What are the next key advancements in robotics?

By 2126, some visions imagine humanoid robots performing household tasks, caring for elderly people, constructing buildings, farming, repairing infrastructure, and working alongside humans.

Other Ideas People in 2026 Are Imagining

The ideas above are some of the biggest recurring visions of the future. But they are far from the only ones.

Across futurism, science, technology, entertainment, and everyday conversations, people are imagining many other possibilities for 2126.

Vertical cities
Cities built upward into enormous multifunctional structures containing homes, workplaces, entertainment, transportation, and other services.
Ocean settlements
Floating or underwater communities designed to create additional living space or adapt to environmental change.
Underground cities
Large underground environments designed for protection from extreme temperatures, radiation, or other environmental pressures.
Fusion power
Commercial fusion reactors providing large amounts of low-carbon energy.
Near-total renewable energy
Electricity systems dominated by solar, wind, storage, nuclear, geothermal, and other low-carbon sources.
Space-based solar power
Large solar installations in space transmitting energy back to Earth or powering infrastructure in orbit.
Asteroid mining
Robotic systems extracting water and metals from asteroids for use in space.
Fully autonomous transportation
Cars, buses, aircraft, ships, and delivery systems operating with little or no human control.
AI-generated entertainment
Movies, games, music, and interactive experiences generated specifically for individual people.
Virtual worlds
Persistent digital environments becoming major places for entertainment, education, social interaction, and work.
Automated agriculture
Robots and AI managing planting, harvesting, monitoring, and other agricultural tasks.
Lab-grown food
Cultivated meat and other laboratory-produced foods becoming normal parts of everyday diets.
Genetically engineered organisms
Plants, animals, and microorganisms deliberately designed for specific purposes.
Human genetic enhancement
Genetic technologies being used not only to treat disease but potentially to alter human abilities.
Digital preservation of people
Extremely detailed digital records of people's lives, memories, personalities, or communication styles.
Direct neural communication
Technologies allowing information to move between brains and computers, or potentially between people, without conventional speech or text.
Shorter workweeks
Automation reducing the amount of time humans need to spend working.
Post-scarcity economies
Automation making many physical goods and services extremely inexpensive or abundant.
Automated construction
Robots and 3D-printing systems constructing homes, infrastructure, and eventually entire settlements.
Artificial ecosystems
Humans deliberately designing or rebuilding ecosystems to maintain biodiversity and environmental stability.

These are not predictions from 2126. They are predictions from 2026.

A Snapshot of 2026

The future imagined by people in 2026 is shaped by the technology, problems, discoveries, fears, and ambitions of the time.

We imagine AI becoming more capable because AI is rapidly becoming more capable around us.

We imagine cities adapting to climate change because climate change is already affecting the way cities are planned.

We imagine humans living in space because governments and private companies are actively working toward expanding human activity beyond Earth.

We imagine longer lives because scientists are studying aging.

We imagine robots everywhere because robots are already moving from controlled industrial environments into the physical world around us.

In 2126, some of these ideas may seem obvious. Others may seem completely strange. Some may have happened almost exactly as imagined. Others may have disappeared from human memory.

But that is not what this archive is trying to judge.

This is simply a record of what people in 2026 could imagine.

This is what we thought the future might look like.
